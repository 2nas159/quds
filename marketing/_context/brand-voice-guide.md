# ملحمة القدس — Brand Voice Guide

## Overall Tone

Warm, familiar, and trustworthy — like a neighbor you already trust, not a corporate brand. Egyptian-Arabic leaning register that feels approachable to the broader Arabic-speaking community in Istanbul (Egyptian, Syrian, Jordanian, Gulf, etc.).

## Core Voice Principles

1. **Warm, not corporate.** Avoid stiff, formal marketing language. Write like a person talking to a customer, not a company issuing a statement.
2. **Confident, not defensive.** When addressing pricing questions or trust concerns, respond with clear, matter-of-fact confidence — never sound evasive or apologetic.
3. **Direct CTA, always.** Every piece of content should make it obvious what to do next: message on WhatsApp.
4. **Masculine verb forms when referring to Enes.** Enes is male — always use masculine Arabic grammatical forms when writing in his voice or referring to him directly (never assume or default to feminine forms).

## Language Guidelines

- Primary language: Arabic (Egyptian-leaning colloquial, broadly understandable across dialects)
- Secondary language: Turkish, used for:
  - Comment replies to Turkish-speaking customers
  - Any bilingual materials (e.g., flyers)
- Keep sentences short and conversational — avoid long, formal paragraphs.
- Emojis are welcome in moderation to add warmth (🥩 🔥 📲 ✅) — do not overuse to the point of looking unprofessional.

## Comment Reply Strategy

- Always redirect pricing questions to WhatsApp, but never in a way that feels like the price is being hidden or withheld as a "secret."
- Give a **reason**, not a deflection — e.g., pricing depends on weight/cut, or is updated regularly to guarantee the best price — rather than simply saying "message us."
- Maintain a professional, warm tone even when a customer is skeptical, confused, or slightly rude. Never sound defensive.
- Personalize replies where possible — reference the customer's specific question rather than a copy-paste generic reply.

## Trust & Quality Messaging

- Emphasize hygiene, quality control, and freshness.
- Never mention sourcing from a market (see `brand-content.md` for the hard rule).
- When addressing halal/quality concerns, reassure customers that the butchery does its own cutting under controlled, hygienic conditions — this is a genuine trust builder and should be used confidently.

## Sample Phrases (reusable building blocks)

- "طازج لبيتك" — Fresh to your home
- "الدفع عند الاستلام" — Cash on delivery
- "توصيل سريع لباب بيتك" — Fast delivery to your door
- "اطلب دلوقتي على الواتساب" — Order now on WhatsApp
- "بنجهزلك طلبك بعناية" — We prepare your order with care

## What to Avoid

- Overly formal/classical Arabic (فصحى) that feels distant or stiff
- Aggressive sales language ("لن تجد أفضل من هذا أبداً")
- Any reference to sourcing meat from a market
- Feminine verb forms when writing as/about Enes
- Long unbroken paragraphs — break into short, scannable lines
